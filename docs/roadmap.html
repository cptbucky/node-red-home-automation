Component roadmap

Milestone badges report delivery progress, independently of the release badges in the portfolio below. Candidate components keep transport, policy, observed state and recovery explicit before joining the package.

Contract examples

Transport-neutral examples cover room lighting, occupancy, switch gestures, source readiness, volume ownership and coordinated activities.

Milestone complete

Volume ownership

Absolute and relative requests share one bounded command owner, confirm from fresh evidence and preserve external adjustments.

Milestone complete

Switch-state observability

Wall-input reducers preserve restart baselines, distinguish retained replay, refresh duplicate evidence and expose freshness or explicit unavailability.

Milestone complete

Home Assistant integration

Home Assistant is an optional view and control panel: each node can publish its state and announce entities, and Home Assistant controls send MQTT messages that the nodes act on, never commands to devices. Entities report availability, keep stable identifiers through renames, and are cleaned up when their node is removed. Rooms expose circadian and sleep switches and wake and bed times; occupancy, motion, receiver volume and covers have their own entities.

Milestone complete

Blind and cover control

Covers have their own nodes. A cover controller owns one blind, shutter or curtain motor: positions and named presets, open/close-only motors judged honestly from what they report, a moving signal other policies can react to, and a person's move left where they put it. A cover group moves several covers as one and says which confirmed. Each cover can also appear in Home Assistant as a cover entity, whose controls come back to the cover node as requests.

Next

Device-agnostic core

Decision logic is separate from devices. Controllers speak a neutral vocabulary of requests and observations, with lighting in percent and kelvin and receiver volume in dB, and device protocols sit in their own modules and adapters. Defaults name no rooms or devices, and powered devices are judged from observed power, read from any meter or adapter. Still to do: moving the display controller's own device calls into adapters, removing a setting shared between nodes behind the scenes, and simplifying the wiring between controllers and the devices they confirm.

Next

Device adapters

Small device nodes that connect the controllers to real devices over MQTT, so a relay module in cover mode or a Zigbee2MQTT cover wires straight in without function nodes. Each adapter knows one kind of device, and the controllers stay device-neutral.

Planned

Adaptive covers

A policy that decides when covers move, from window contacts, a schedule, an enable switch and readings such as sun or temperature, and asks the cover nodes to move them. A person's move is respected, restarts and repeated messages are handled, and a cover never closes onto an open window.

Milestone complete

Scenes with restore

A scene holds a room's lights on or off and pauses motion lighting. Ending it, by request, a safety timer or a trigger, hands back every light: one the room was running returns to the room, and one that was off or set by hand gets its values back. A person's change during the scene is kept. Scenes stack: the newest holds shared lights and hands them back to the one beneath.

Queued

Grouped audio

Model membership, synchronization and partial failure without hiding which players are unavailable.

Planned

Water irrigation

Run garden zones from schedules and soil or rain evidence, one valve owner per zone. Each run is bounded, confirmed from the valve's observed state, and skipped or cut short when rain or moisture says it isn't needed. A person's manual run takes over until it ends.

Planned

Heating and climate

Hold each room at a scheduled temperature from observed temperature and valve state. A person's manual change wins until the next schedule step, and an open window pauses heating instead of fighting it.

Planned

Humidity-driven ventilation

Run extractor fans in bathrooms and kitchens from a rise in humidity and stop them once it falls back, with a bounded run time, a manual boost and a short overrun.

Planned

Water leak protection

Close a main water valve when a leak sensor trips and confirm it closed from the valve's observed state. The alarm stays latched until a person clears it, and an unconfirmed close is reported, never assumed.

Planned

Energy-aware scheduling

Run deferrable loads, such as a dishwasher, water heating or vehicle charging, in the cheapest or greenest window from tariff or solar data, finishing before a deadline the user sets.
