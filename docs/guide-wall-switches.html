Wire wall switches

Turn wall switch flips and button presses into lighting requests through the switch lighting policy node. A single press toggles the room’s lights; a double press sets them to full brightness and marks them as set by a person.

Before you wire it

This guide adds a wall switch or button to a room that already has a room lighting controller (home-room-lighting-controller). The switch goes to its own node, the switch lighting policy (home-switch-lighting-policy, shown on the canvas as switch policy). It turns what the switch reports into msg.lighting intents for the room controller. It knows nothing about occupancy or motion.

Every message names its kind in msg.source. The policy accepts three kinds:

Switch messages the policy accepts
msg.sourcemsg.payloadUse it for
"switch"{state: true} or {state: false}A wall switch that reports its position (raw edges). The state must be a boolean; anything else is ignored.
"switch-action""single" or "double", or an object with that actionA button that already reports which press it saw (pre-classified actions). Case does not matter.
"switch-availability"true, "online", "available" or "on"; false, "offline", "unavailable" or "off"Whether the switch device is reachable.

A raw switch report can carry the time it was observed in msg.observedAt or payload.observedAt. Without one, the policy uses the time the message arrived; a retained report without one has no time, so the status shows stale with age ?.

The policy commands nothing itself. The room controller sends the light commands, straight to the lights over MQTT. Home Assistant is not in this path.

Wire the switch to the room

One switch policy feeds one room controller. Convert your switch’s reports into one of the messages above on the way in.

Connections
FromConnect toWhy
Switch reports, convertedswitch policy inputmsg.source = "switch" with a boolean state, or "switch-action" with the press.
Switch availability, convertedswitch policy inputmsg.source = "switch-availability", so the policy knows when the switch is offline.
switch policy outputRoom lighting controller inputThe policy’s only output carries the msg.lighting intents.
Room lighting controller output 2Each light’s adapter (a zigbee2mqtt light)The light commands. Each adapter publishes them through its Broker, and tells the room’s commands from other people’s by watching the light’s /set topic. See Wire the room.

A raw switch needs its report turned into a boolean. In a Function node between MQTT in and the policy:

Function → switch policy · raw switch report
msg.source = "switch";
msg.payload = {state: msg.payload.state === "<your device’s on value>"};
return msg;

A button whose message already has an action of single or double needs only the source set:

Function → switch policy · pre-classified button
msg.source = "switch-action";
return msg;
  1. Match the targets.

    Set the policy’s Targets to the ids of the room controller targets the switch controls. The default is ["main"].

  2. Keep the window short.

    Leave Raw-edge window ms at 500 unless two quick flips are not being seen as a double press.

  3. Try it without a switch.

    The Room lighting example has a “wall switch edge” Inject node that sends msg.source = "switch" with {"state":true} into a switch policy wired to a room controller.

Wire a Shelly relay behind a wall switch

A common way to keep smart bulbs on a wall switch: put a Shelly Gen2-or-later relay behind the switch, keep the relay on so the bulb always has power, and detach the switch from the relay. Each press then goes out over MQTT, for Node-RED to act on, instead of cutting the bulb’s power.

Mains wiring is dangerous. Leave it to a qualified electrician.

Wiring a relay into a light circuit means working on mains voltage, and local regulations say who may do it and how. Switch off the circuit breaker and check there is no voltage at the wires before touching them. Shelly’s own diagram and instructions for your exact model are the authority; the drawing below only shows the idea.

  • The wall switch doesn’t cut power. The relay stays on, so the bulb holder stays powered whatever the switch’s position. To cut power to the light, for example to change a bulb, switch the relay off from the Shelly’s own app or web page, or from Home Assistant if it shows the relay. For any work on the fitting or the wiring, switch off the circuit breaker.
  • Keep within the relay’s rating. Shelly gives the 1PM Gen3 a maximum switching current of 16 A at 240 V~, and asks for a circuit breaker of at most 16 A rated current. Check the rating for your model and the load on the circuit.
  • The switch works only while Node-RED and MQTT do. If Node-RED, the MQTT broker or the relay’s network connection is down, pressing the wall switch does nothing: the bulb stays as it was, and keeps its power. The fallback script below makes the switch work as an ordinary switch then.
  • Don’t use the wall switch to change a bulb. With the fallback script, a wall switch that cut the power while Node-RED was away has the relay switched back on as soon as Node-RED returns, so the bulb holder can come back on in your hands. Switch the relay off from its app or web page, where it stays off, or switch off the circuit breaker.
  1. Detach the switch from the relay.

    In the device’s web interface or app, set the output type to Detached Switch (in the API, the switch’s in_mode is detached). The input then no longer changes the relay, so a press can’t cut the bulb’s power.

  2. Say what kind of switch it is.

    Set the input mode to Switch for a toggle switch that stays where you put it, or Button for a momentary push button (the input’s type is switch or button). A toggle switch reports its on/off position; a button reports pushes, such as single_push, double_push and long_push.

  3. Keep the relay on.

    Switch the output on, and set Action on power on to Turn ON (initial_state on), so the bulb has power again after a power cut rather than waiting for someone to switch the relay back on.

  4. Turn on MQTT.

    Under Settings → Connectivity → MQTT, enable MQTT and set your broker as the server; the device restarts to apply it. Leave RPC status notifications over MQTT on (the default): input changes and button pushes arrive on <prefix>/events/rpc, and the device reports true or false on <prefix>/online. The topic prefix is the device ID unless you set your own; note it.

  5. Bring the presses into Node-RED.

    Add a shelly switch node. Set its Broker, its MQTT topic prefix to the device’s prefix, its Switch ID to 0 (or the switch your wall switch is wired to), and leave Mode on reading the device’s own setup. It subscribes to <prefix>/# and publishes to the device itself. Wire its second output into this guide’s switch policy. (A node saved before 6.2 keeps its MQTT in node on <prefix>/# and its first output to MQTT out until you choose a Broker.)

    Each time the broker connects, and whenever the device comes online, the node asks the device how it is set up, and its status shows what it found, such as detached with a switch or a button. It then sends the policy a toggle switch’s flips as edges, with the device’s status first as the baseline so the first flip after a deploy counts as a press, a button’s single, double and long pushes as actions, and the device going offline or online as availability. The switch policy acts on single and double pushes; a long push reaches it but does nothing yet. The node never commands a detached relay. If the relay reports off, for example after a fallback press below, its status says the bulbs have lost power. If the device’s setup can’t be read, the status says so and nothing is passed on until you set Mode to detached. See Wire a shelly switch for its settings.

Sources: Shelly’s knowledge base for the 1PM Gen3 (wiring, rating and safety), the 1 Gen3 web interface guide (setting names), and the Gen2 API for Switch, Input, MQTT, notifications and RPC channels.

When the relay is the light

With ordinary bulbs, the relay itself switches the light. Set the device up as above, but leave out keeping the relay on: the room switches it. The shelly switch node is then both the wall switch’s adapter and the light’s.

  1. Add the light to the room.

    In the room controller’s Targets JSON, give the relay an id and mark it on/off only: [{"id": "main", "onOffOnly": true}].

  2. Give the shelly switch node that Target.

    Set its Target to main. Wire the room controller’s second output (its light commands) into the node, and the node’s third output back into the room. The second output still carries the presses to the switch policy, whose intents go to the room.

  3. Press the switch.

    The press reaches the room as a person’s intent; the room sends the light on or off; the node switches the relay and reports what it did. Motion, scenes and activities switch the same relay through the room. A change made elsewhere, such as the Shelly app, reaches the room as a person’s.

The Shelly switch example (Import → Examples) wires exactly this, through one broker config node whose host you set.

Keep the wall switch working without Node-RED

A small script on the relay covers the times Node-RED can’t answer. While the relay’s MQTT connection is down, or Node-RED has announced itself offline, a press at the wall toggles the relay, cutting or restoring the bulb’s power like an ordinary switch. When the connection is back and Node-RED announces itself online, the script switches the relay back on if a fallback press switched it off, so the bulb has power again for Node-RED to control. A relay a person switched off, from the app or the web page, stays off. The rest of the time the script never touches the relay.

First, tell Node-RED to announce itself.

Open the MQTT broker node your flows use and, on its messages settings, set a Birth message with payload online, and a Close message and Will message with payload offline, all on the same topic (for example node-red/status) and all retained. The broker sends the will message for Node-RED when its connection drops, and the retained message tells the script where things stand whenever it subscribes. Without these messages the script can tell only when the relay loses MQTT, not when Node-RED stops.

  1. Add the script to the relay.

    In the relay’s web interface, open Scripts, choose Create script, give it a name, and paste in wall-switch-fallback.js. MQTT must be on, as set up above.

  2. Set it up.

    At the top of the script, set nodeRedTopic to the broker node’s topic and nodeRedOnline to its birth payload, and inputId and switchId if your wall switch isn’t on input 0 and switch 0.

  3. Start it, and start it with the relay.

    Choose Save, then Start, and in the scripts list turn on Run on startup. The console below the editor says which topic the script is waiting for, warns if nothing has arrived on it after a minute, and shows when the fallback starts and ends.

What it can’t see

The script knows only whether the relay reaches the broker and what Node-RED last announced. If Node-RED is up but the flow that handles the switch isn’t working, presses still go to Node-RED and do nothing. Until anything arrives on the topic, after the script starts or if the broker has lost its retained messages, it falls back only while the relay’s MQTT connection is down. A full deploy in Node-RED sends the close message and then the birth message, so presses toggle the relay for those few seconds. In the fallback, a toggle switch’s position no longer says whether the light is on: each flip toggles it. For any work on the fitting or the wiring, switch off the circuit breaker.

Script: shelly/wall-switch-fallback.js, tested in the package’s suite against a simulated device. Sources: Shelly’s scripting overview, tutorial (adding a script), language reference, the Shelly, MQTT and Timer script APIs, and the Switch component (Switch.Toggle’s was_on).

What each press requests

With raw edges, the policy times changes of the switch’s state. A change starts the raw-edge window. A second change inside it is a double press; when the window ends with no second change, it is a single press. A report that repeats the current state is not a press.

Presses and requests
PressRaw switchPre-classified actionIntent sent
SingleOne change, no second within the window"single"state: "TOGGLE"
DoubleTwo changes within the window"double"state: "ON", manual: true, values: {brightnessPct: 100}
Anything else, such as a hold—Other actionsNothing; traced as unsupported-action

Every intent names the policy’s targets and carries source: "wall" and person: true. A double action on the default target sends:

switch policy → room lighting controller · double press
msg.lighting = {
  kind: "intent",
  targets: ["main"],
  state: "ON",
  source: "wall",
  person: true,
  manual: true,
  values: {brightnessPct: 100}
};

The room controller resolves TOGGLE per light: off if it last set or saw the light on, otherwise on. A light switched on by a single press gets the room’s style (from a circadian lighting policy) when the room has one and the light is under its control.

A pre-classified action cancels a raw press still waiting for its window to end.

Presses, motion and manual lights

Because every press carries person: true, the room controller treats it as a person’s choice:

  • Motion keeps its hands off. The motion policy’s vacancy OFF carries vacantSince. The room controller ignores it for a light a person set after the room went vacant (traced as person-set-after-vacancy).
  • Presses reach held lights. A light held by an owner, such as an activity, ignores owner-less intents like motion, but a press still reaches it.
  • A double press makes the light manual. It sends manual: true, so the room’s style then leaves it alone. Switching circadian lighting on returns manual lights to the room’s control.
  • Switching off hands the light back. A light switched off by a person or its owner returns to in-control.
Switches don’t go to the motion policy.

Switch messages sent to the motion lighting policy are ignored. Wire switches only to the switch policy.

Retained reports and restarts

The policy keeps the switch’s last known state (its baseline) in the node’s context, so a flip after a restart is still measured against it.

  • Retained reports are not presses. A raw report with msg.retain = true only re-baselines the state. A retained switch-action is ignored.
  • Mark a first read and polled snapshots. Set msg.baseline = true on a report you asked the device for, so it updates the baseline without counting as a press.
  • With no baseline, the first report is a press. On a policy that does not yet know the switch’s state, the first report that is neither retained nor marked baseline counts as a change and sends a single-press TOGGLE. Mark a first read with msg.baseline = true.
  • Offline means start again. After the switch reports unavailable, its first report when it returns re-baselines instead of counting as a press.

Node context is kept in memory unless you set up a context store, so a restart or redeploy clears the baseline, and the room controller forgets which lights a person set. See Recommended setup: a file context store.

A wall switch through the shelly switch node

A wall switch wired to a Gen2-or-later relay in detached mode keeps the relay on to power smart bulbs and reports each press over MQTT. The shelly switch node turns those reports into this policy’s inputs, with no function nodes: a toggle switch’s flips as edges (the device’s status first, as the baseline), a push button’s presses as actions, and the device going offline or online as availability. It reads the device’s mode and input type itself. To set up the relay and wire the node, see Wire a Shelly relay behind a wall switch and Wire a shelly switch.

Stop or open a cover from a button

The switch policy only makes lighting intents. To make a button stop a moving cover, or open it otherwise, send its request to the cover controller with msg.stopIfMoving = true. See Leave a person’s move alone in the covers guide.

Check the result

The policy’s status shows how much it trusts what it knows about the switch.

Read the status
What you seeWhat it means
switch fresh · <age>The switch reported within the freshness time.
switch stale · <age>The last report is older than the freshness time, has no usable timestamp (shown as age ?), or came before the flows started and nothing has confirmed it since.
awaiting switch baselineThe policy does not yet know the switch’s state.
switch unavailableThe switch was reported unavailable.

Connect a Debug node to the policy’s output and press the switch: a single press shows a TOGGLE intent; a double press shows the full-brightness ON intent. Then check the room controller’s commands reach the light.
