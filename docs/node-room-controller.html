Room lighting controller

Message shapes, output and settings for the node that owns one room's lights. It turns intents into light commands, tracks who controls each light, and applies the style it is sent.

home-room-lighting-controller1 input · 3 outputshome automation

One room. One owner of its light commands.

The room lighting controller turns intents from policies (motion, wall switches, activities) into light commands, keeps track of who controls each light, and styles the lights it controls with the style it is sent. In the palette it is room lighting controller (home-room-lighting-controller); on the canvas it shows its name, or “<area> lighting controller”.

A room without a circadian lighting policy styles nothing: its lights just go on and off.

Looking for wiring?

Follow Room lighting for a working room, and Motion and occupancy for the motion path into it.

Inputs

Policies send msg.lighting. Each light’s reports, and other people’s commands on it, arrive from its adapter (such as a zigbee2mqtt light) as msg.light readings: see the neutral light contract. A raw MQTT message is ignored.

Intent with an owner · msg.lighting
{
  kind: "intent",
  targets: ["wall", "lamp"],
  state: "ON",
  source: "activity",
  owner: "theatre",
  values: {brightnessPct: 25, colorTempKelvin: 2500},
  preserveManual: true
}

brightnessPct: 0 is the dimmest the light goes, not off: send state: "OFF" to switch it off. A value that isn’t a number, or a colour temperature of 0 or less, is left out with a warning. Zigbee2MQTT’s own brightness and color_temp were removed in 6.0: they are left out with a warning once per deploy.

Circadian, sleep and schedule messages go to the circadian lighting policy since 6.0; the room ignores them, with a warning.

Don’t rewrite the lights’ commands.

Each light’s adapter watches the light’s command path to tell the room’s commands from other people’s, so a function that rewrites them on the way makes them look like someone else’s. A bare ON or OFF that arrives within 5 seconds of one of the room’s commands with that power, before that command has come back, is taken as that command (the room traces mutated-own-command), and the room restyles a light after someone else’s bare ON at most once every 5 seconds.

The neutral light contract

The room speaks to each light through an adapter on msg.light: commands out (port 2), readings in. The zigbee2mqtt light node is one adapter; any flow that answers the same messages works in its place, for a light that isn’t Zigbee2MQTT’s. Values are brightness in percent and colour temperature in kelvin, and every message names the light’s target id.

An adapter judges equality in its device’s own units; the room never compares values. One that can’t ask its light for a reading ignores observe. The README’s Neutral light contract has every message in full.

Settings from before 6.0

A 5.x room’s Targets JSON named each light’s Zigbee2MQTT topics (stateTopic, commandTopic), which move to a zigbee2mqtt light per light; its dialog lists them and can create the light nodes, and saving keeps only each light’s id. Circadian lighting moved to the circadian lighting policy in 6.0. The room keeps its old circadian settings only to carry them to that migration, with no form rows: maxBrightnessPct, maxColorTempKelvin, sleepBrightnessPct, sleepColorTempKelvin, refreshTransitionSeconds, refreshSeconds, initialSleep, wakeTime, bedTime and houseSchedule.

A room that still has any of them, or a target with topics, is an invalid configuration, and its lights don’t respond, until it is migrated. Its edit dialog lists the old values; add a circadian lighting policy wired to the room with those values, then save the room’s dialog, which clears them. A room that should stay unstyled needs only the save. See the 6.0.0 upgrade notes.

Control state

Each target has one control state.

  • in-control: the room styles the light with the style it is sent. This is the default.
  • overridden: another owner, such as an activity or a scene, holds the light on or off. Styles and owner-less intents leave it alone until it is released.
  • manual: a person set the light: an intent with manual: true (a wall switch’s double press), or someone else’s command on it with values, as its adapter reports it. An intent’s values alone don’t make a light manual. Switching circadian lighting on returns manual lights to in-control.

An overridden light changes only for an intent with an owner (its own, or a newer owner who takes it over), a person (manual: true, person: true, someone else’s command on the light as its adapter reports it, or a change seen at the light), or a release. Any other intent, including motion ON or OFF, leaves it alone and is traced as ignored held-by. A person switching a held light off returns it to in-control; a person switching on a light an owner holds off makes it manual. Either way it leaves every owner, and their later releases leave it alone.

Owners stack

The first owner to take a light records what it was: its control state, whether it was on, and the brightness and colour temperature it last reported. A newer owner goes on top and sets the light; the owner beneath keeps its own request, and sending again updates it without changing the light. The owners, and what each light was, are kept with the room’s state, so they survive a redeploy with a context store.

  • Releasing the owner on top hands the light to the owner beneath, with what that owner last asked for.
  • Releasing an owner beneath a newer one just drops it; the light is left as it is.
  • When the last owner lets go (or on a release without owner), the light goes back to what was recorded. A light the room was running is the room’s again, on or off as it was: on, it takes the room’s style as it is now. A light that was off goes off. A light a person had set gets the values it last reported, and stays manual.

A motion OFF intent carries vacantSince, so a light a person set after the room went vacant stays on.

Control states are kept in the node’s context. When the node starts with nothing saved (its first deploy, or a redeploy or restart without a context store), it leaves every light as it is until an intent names it, someone switches it on with a bare ON, or circadian lighting is switched on. See the recommended context store.

Style

The room keeps the latest style it was sent and uses it when a light it controls comes on with no values given (a motion ON, a wall-switch press), on each new style for lights that are on, after someone else’s bare ON, and when the last owner releases a light the room was running. A style never switches a light on or off and never reaches a light that an owner holds or a person set. With no style, or once a style has run out (until), the lights just go on and off.

Style · msg.lighting
{
  kind: "style",
  values: {brightnessPct, colorTempKelvin} | null,
  transitionSeconds,
  until

Optional MQTT and Home Assistant

New nodes start with Don’t publish. Otherwise, topics are named after the Area: Guest Room publishes under home/guest_room/….

With a Broker, the room publishes itself, through Node-RED’s MQTT broker node: no MQTT out on output 1 and no Status node are needed, and both can be deleted. Each time the broker connects, reconnects included, and each time Home Assistant comes back (online on <prefix>/status, its birth message), the room clears what it no longer publishes and announces everything again: its entities, its availability and every light’s control state. So entities come back by themselves after a broker or Home Assistant restart. Output 1 sends nothing, so an MQTT out still wired to it goes quiet, and a Status node still wired in is ignored, with a trace. As it stops, it sends offline; deleted, it clears what it published. Without a Broker, everything below goes out on output 1, as before.

Published topics
TopicRetainedPayload
home/<area>/lighting/<target>/controlYes{"state": "in-control" | "overridden" | "manual", "owner": string | null, "available"?: boolean}, at startup and whenever a target’s control state or availability changes. available appears once the light’s adapter has reported it.
home/<area>/lighting/availabilityYesonline once the flows start, offline as it stops.

The availability messages get through only when this node is stopped on its own, so set the MQTT broker node’s Birth, Close and Will messages to the same. With discovery on, Home Assistant gets a diagnostic sensor per light showing its control state, with the owner as an attribute. The entities are announced once the flows start, before online, and again at the startup message; switching discovery off removes them at the next startup message. At every start the room also clears the circadian, sleep, wake and bed entities it published before 6.0. Home Assistant shows state here; the room never commands lights through it. See Home Assistant.

Status

The node status reads styled or unstyled, then how many lights are on, how many are manual, how many are overridden when any are, and how many lights are offline when any are (as their adapters report), for example styled · 2 on · 0 manual · 1 offline. The dot is yellow while any light is manual, green while styled, and grey otherwise.
