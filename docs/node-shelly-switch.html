One switch channel, used as the device is set up

The shelly switch node (type home-shelly-switch; canvas label: its name, or “shelly switch”) connects one switch channel of a Shelly Gen2-or-later relay. It reads the device’s own configuration for the channel and works in one of two ways: detached, where the wall switch’s presses go to a switch lighting policy and the relay keeps smart bulbs powered, or relay, where the relay is the light. With a Target, the relay is a room light in either mode, and the node is its adapter on the neutral light contract: the room switches the relay, and sees its state (see As a room light). It has no broker connection of its own: device messages arrive from an MQTT in node, and its publications leave through an MQTT out node. No function nodes, no Home Assistant and no HTTP.

Looking for wiring?

Follow Wire a shelly switch, then the wall switches guide for the switch policy.

Settings

Editor settings and exact defaults

Namename · stringEmptyDisplay label.
Brokerbroker · mqtt-brokerNoneNode-RED’s MQTT broker node. With one, the node subscribes to its device’s topics (<prefix>/#) and publishes on them itself: output 1 sends nothing, and the MQTT in and MQTT out nodes around it are no longer needed (still wired, a device message on its input is ignored, with a trace, so nothing is doubled). Its own commands come back through its subscription, as through an MQTT in node before, so it still tells them from someone else’s. Needed for a new node; opening and saving the node offers the first broker in your flows. A node saved before 6.2 keeps using its MQTT in and out nodes, and needs no broker until you choose one (publishesThroughOutput, with no form row, true for it and cleared when a broker is chosen).

Modemode · enumautoauto (“As the device is set up”) reads the device’s configuration; detached or relay override it, and are needed when it can’t be read.

On the device, MQTT must be on with Enable MQTT Control and RPC status notifications (both the default).

Mode

With auto, the device’s in_mode for switch:<id> decides: detached means detached, and every other mode (follow, flip, momentary and the rest) means relay. The input’s type (switch or button) decides what detached mode emits.

What each mode emits on output 2
ModeEventMessage
Detached, switch-type inputInput on or offmsg.source = "switch", msg.payload = {state}; every reading from the device’s whole status carries baseline: true, while a change notification does not
Detached, button-type inputsingle_push, double_push, long_pushmsg.source = "switch-action", msg.payload = {action} with single, double or long. The switch policy acts on single and double; long is passed on for later use. Other events are ignored.
DetachedDevice offline or onlinemsg.source = "switch-availability", msg.payload "online" or "offline"
RelayRelay output reportedmsg.switchState = {kind: "observed", on, person, observedAt}; person is true when the device names its own input as the source of the change
RelayDevice offline or onlinemsg.switchState = {kind: "availability", online}

In detached mode without a Target, the relay is never switched from here. If it reports off, the status warns that the bulbs have lost power.

As a room light

With a Target, the relay is that room light, whether the device is detached (the wall switch’s presses still go to the switch policy, and the room switches the relay) or not (the relay follows the switch, and the room can switch it too). The node speaks the room’s neutral light contract, like the zigbee2mqtt light:

  • Commands: the room’s ON or OFF for its Target becomes on or off on <prefix>/command/switch:<id>. A relay has no brightness, colour or fade, so those are left out: mark the target "onOffOnly": true, and the room never sends them. An observe becomes status_update there. A restyle of a relay already on is answered skipped.
  • Readings on output 3: observed with the relay’s output (fields: []; showsLast when it matches the last command sent), each time it reports.
  • A person’s change: external when the relay’s output changes with none of this node’s commands pending (a person at a switch the relay follows, the Shelly app), or when someone else’s on, off or toggle appears on the relay’s command topic. Its own commands’ echoes there are recognised as its own. The room may take an external change as a person’s.
  • Availability: {kind: "availability", target, online}, shown by the room, never acted on.

msg.switchState and msg.switchCommand in relay mode work as before, for flows that use them.

The reply is addressed under the device’s prefix, so the same MQTT in node on <prefix>/# receives it.

Startup and availability

Once the flows have started, and whenever the device comes back online, the node sends Shelly.GetConfig and status_update. If no configuration arrives within 10 seconds, a configuration read earlier is kept; if none was read, it is taken as unreadable, and with Mode on auto nothing is passed on until Mode says how. Offline and online are passed on as shown under Mode, and relay commands are refused while offline.

Status

  • reading the device’s configuration (grey ring) while the request is out.
  • configuration unreadable: set Mode (red ring).
  • The mode found, such as detached, switch input (green). With Mode set, it reads detached (set) or relay (set), followed by what the device says, if read.
  • Relay mode adds the relay state: · on or · off; with a Target, the light’s: · hall on.
  • Detached, without a Target, with the relay off adds · relay off: bulbs unpowered (yellow ring).
  • offline (grey ring).
