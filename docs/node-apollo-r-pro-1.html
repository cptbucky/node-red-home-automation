Apollo R PRO-1, as the room takes it

The apollo r pro-1 node (type home-apollo-r-pro-1; canvas label: its name, its device, or “apollo r pro-1”) turns an Apollo R PRO-1’s two radars and light sensor (Apollo Automation’s ESPHome presence sensor) into the messages occupancy fusion and the motion lighting policy take. It subscribes through Node-RED’s MQTT broker node itself: no MQTT in, change or function nodes, and never Home Assistant. Its entities are fixed for this device’s firmware; another ESPHome device gets its own node.

The device: Apollo R PRO-1 (Apollo Automation’s documentation).

Looking for wiring?

Follow Motion and occupancy.

How it finds its entities

From the device’s own MQTT discovery first. ESPHome describes each entity, retained, on <discovery prefix>/<component>/<device>/<entity>/config, with its state topic and device class. The node takes binary sensors of class motion as motion, occupancy or presence as presence, and sensors of class illuminance as the light level, and subscribes to the state topics they name, so a firmware that renames an entity, or a custom topic prefix, still works. Where discovery and the entity names below differ, discovery wins, and the difference is traced.

  • The model must match: the model in the device’s discovery must be the Apollo R PRO-1. Another model’s discovery is ignored, and the status says so.
  • Without discovery, the node uses the entity names below. To turn it on, set mqtt: discovery: true in the device’s ESPHome configuration (ESPHome’s default; some device configurations turn it off) and flash it again.
  • The status ends with discovered or preset.

What it reads without discovery

It has two radars, an LD2450 and an LD2412, and no PIR. Each role is on when any radar that has reported says so, and off when every one that has reported says off. Apollo’s firmware has named the LD2450’s entities both ways: without a prefix (presence, moving_target) on earlier releases, and with ld2450_ on current ones. The node reads both, and an entity your firmware never publishes doesn’t count.

Outputs

Two output ports
PortMessageConnect to
1 · Motion and presence{source: "motion" | "presence", payload: true | false}, with baseline: true at a startOccupancy fusion’s input.

These are fixed: output 1 carries only booleans from ON and OFF, output 2 only a number. Anything else, such as nan, is ignored with a trace and never passed on. Only a change is sent.

As Node-RED starts

ESPHome keeps every state retained, so the broker hands the node the device’s states as it subscribes. While the device’s status is online, they are its current state, and the node passes motion and presence on with baseline: true (presence first):

  • Someone there: occupancy fusion marks the room occupied and sends nothing, so no light is switched on; lights stay as they are, and motion acts from its next change.
  • Room empty: fusion sends occupied: false, so the motion policy starts its vacancy countdown as if the room had just emptied, and the lights go off when it runs out.
  • Device not online: its retained states are ignored. States that arrive before its status are held until it says online.
