Share a house schedule

Give many rooms one wake time and one bed time. Each circadian lighting policy follows the house schedule without wires, and an optional MQTT node lets Home Assistant change the times.

Before you wire it

A circadian lighting policy styles the rooms it is wired to by a wake time and a bed time (default 07:00 and 23:00). Levels rise to the daytime level over the hour after waking, stay at the daytime brightness and colour through the day, wind down to the sleep settings over the three hours before bed, and stay at the sleep settings until you wake. Without a schedule, every policy keeps its own two times.

The house schedule (home-house-schedule) holds one wake and bed time for every circadian lighting policy that uses it. It is a configuration node, like an MQTT broker: it has no wires and does not sit on the canvas. You choose it in each policy’s Schedule setting, and it appears with the editor’s other configuration nodes. The house schedule owns when (wake and bed); each policy owns the levels over the day and its own sleep mode.

  • You need at least one circadian lighting policy already wired into a room lighting controller.
  • Times are 24-hour HH:MM in the Node-RED host’s local time. Docker containers default to UTC: set the container’s TZ if the host should follow a local timezone.
  • The house schedule MQTT node is optional. Policies follow the schedule without it; add it when you want the times on MQTT or in Home Assistant.
House schedule settings
SettingDefaultWhat it does
NamehouseLabels the schedule and names its topics, home/<name>/schedule/.... Needs a letter or digit. In topics the name is lower-cased and other characters become _: Main House publishes on home/main_house/schedule/....
Wake time07:00When the rooms start to brighten.
Bed time23:00When the rooms reach the sleep settings. It must differ from the wake time.

A bed time after midnight, or a wake time later than the bed time, works.

Share one schedule between rooms

Circadian lighting policies reference the schedule; only the optional MQTT node has wires. Each policy hears of every change as it happens and restyles its rooms.

What connects to what
FromConnect toWhy
Circadian lighting policy Schedule settinghouse scheduleThe policy takes its wake and bed times from it. No wire.
house schedule MQTT Schedule settinghouse scheduleRequired. The node publishes and changes this schedule. No wire.
house schedule MQTT Broker settingNode-RED’s MQTT broker nodeIt publishes the times, availability and Home Assistant discovery, and reads home/<name>/schedule/wake-time/set and bed-time/set, itself. No wire. (Without one, as before 6.2: an MQTT in node on those topics into it, and its output to MQTT out.)
  1. Add the schedule from a policy.

    Open a circadian lighting policy (home-circadian-lighting-policy) and set Schedule. Use the editor’s add button to create a house schedule, or pick one that already exists; the editor offers an existing schedule first for a new policy.

  2. Set the times once.

    Edit the house schedule’s Wake time and Bed time. Every policy that uses it follows these times, not its own Wake time and Bed time.

  3. Point the other policies at it.

    Choose the same schedule as Schedule in each policy that should share the times.

  4. Add the MQTT node if you want it.

    Drag a house schedule MQTT node (home-house-schedule-mqtt in the palette) onto the canvas, choose the schedule as its Schedule, and wire it to the broker as in the table. Use one per schedule: two would share the same topics and entities, and deleting one clears them for both until the other next starts.

The importable example House schedule.json shows two areas, each with its own circadian lighting policy and room, sharing one schedule named house, with an Inject node that changes the wake time through the MQTT node.

Change the times

Send changes to the house schedule MQTT node. It changes the shared schedule, and every policy following it restyles its rooms. Give new times as HH:MM, either one alone:

Function → house schedule MQTT · wake earlier
msg.lighting = {kind: "schedule", wakeTime: "06:30"};
return msg;
Function → house schedule MQTT · set both times
msg.lighting = {kind: "schedule", wakeTime: "06:30", bedTime: "22:30"};
return msg;

Times that are not valid HH:MM, or a wake time equal to the bed time, are ignored.

From MQTT, a time on a set topic does the same. A message is read as HH:MM or HH:MM:SS; retained messages and anything but a time are ignored.

House schedule topics (default name house)
TopicDirectionPayload
home/house/schedule/wake-time/setInA new wake time
home/house/schedule/bed-time/setInA new bed time
home/house/schedule/wake-time/stateOut, retainedThe wake time in use, HH:MM:SS
home/house/schedule/bed-time/stateOut, retainedThe bed time in use, HH:MM:SS
home/house/schedule/availabilityOut, retainedonline after the flows start; offline as the node stops (see below)

With Publish set to MQTT state and Home Assistant discovery (the editor’s default for a new node; an imported flow without the setting gets MQTT state), Home Assistant gets a Wake time and a Bed time entity under the Discovery prefix (homeassistant), named after the schedule so a replacement MQTT node keeps them. Changing one in Home Assistant sends it to the set topic, and the node acts on it: with a Broker it reads that itself, and without one through an MQTT in node on its set topics. See Home Assistant for the rest of the setup.

The node’s own offline, and the clearing of its retained topics and entities when you delete it, reach the broker only on a “Modified nodes” or “Modified flows” deploy. On a full deploy, a restart or a crash, Node-RED closes the broker connection at the same moment, so set the MQTT broker node’s Birth message to online and its Close message and Will message to offline, all retained, on the availability topic. See Upgrading from 2.x in the README.

With a Broker, the node announces the times again each time the broker connects, reconnects included. Without one, wire a Status node whose scope is your MQTT out node into it to do the same. You can also send it msg.source = "startup" yourself.

A changed time lasts until the setting changes.

A time set by a message or from Home Assistant stays until a deploy gives the house schedule a different Wake time or Bed time; that setting then applies. Without a file context store, a restart or redeploy also returns the schedule to its settings.

Give a room its own times

A room that needs different times (a child’s room, say) gets its own circadian lighting policy, which sets Schedule to none and keeps its own Wake time and Bed time.

Following a schedule or not
Policy’s ScheduleIts timesIts own wake and bed time topics and entities
A house scheduleThe schedule’s, at every changeIts wake-time/set and bed-time/set topics are ignored; its Home Assistant wake and bed time entities are cleared.
NoneIts own settingsWhile it publishes (Publish set to MQTT state or more), published and changed under home/<area>/wake-time and bed-time, as the README’s Room light control describes.

Sleep stays per policy. A policy’s sleep mode still overrides the schedule, so one area can go to its sleep settings while the others follow the house times.

Deleting a house schedule in the editor sets its policies to none, and they return to their own times rather than keeping the schedule’s last ones.
